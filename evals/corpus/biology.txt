CELL BIOLOGY REVISION NOTES
CHAPTER ONE: PHOTOSYNTHESIS
Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored as glucose.
It occurs in the chloroplasts of plant cells and requires water and carbon
dioxide as raw inputs. The light-dependent reactions take place in the
thylakoid membranes and generate ATP and NADPH.
CHAPTER TWO: CELLULAR RESPIRATION
Cellular respiration releases the energy stored in glucose molecules.
In eukaryotic cells it takes place in the mitochondria and yields
approximately 32 molecules of ATP per molecule of glucose consumed.
Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm and does not require oxygen.
CHAPTER THREE: ENZYMES
Enzymes are biological catalysts that lower the activation energy of a
reaction. Each enzyme is specific to a particular substrate shape.
Enzyme activity is affected by temperature and pH. Most human enzymes
denature above 40 degrees Celsius and lose their catalytic function.
CHAPTER FOUR: CELL DIVISION
Mitosis produces two genetically identical diploid daughter cells.
Meiosis produces four genetically distinct haploid gametes and involves
crossing over during prophase I, which increases genetic variation.
CHAPTER FIVE: MEMBRANE TRANSPORT
Diffusion moves particles from high concentration to low concentration
and does not require energy input from the cell.
Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a semipermeable membrane.
Active transport moves substances against the concentration gradient and
consumes ATP produced by respiration.
Facilitated diffusion uses carrier proteins but still requires no energy.
CHAPTER SIX: GENETICS
DNA is a double helix composed of nucleotide bases adenine, thymine,
guanine and cytosine. Adenine pairs with thymine and guanine pairs
with cytosine through hydrogen bonding.
A gene is a sequence of DNA that codes for a specific polypeptide.
Transcription copies DNA into messenger RNA inside the nucleus.
Translation assembles the polypeptide at the ribosome.
CHAPTER SEVEN: HOMEOSTASIS
Homeostasis maintains a stable internal environment despite external change.
Insulin lowers blood glucose concentration by promoting uptake into cells.
Glucagon raises blood glucose by stimulating the breakdown of glycogen.
Negative feedback loops reverse a deviation from the normal set point.
