MODERN HISTORY STUDY NOTES
UNIT ONE: THE INDUSTRIAL REVOLUTION
The industrial revolution began in Britain in the late eighteenth century.
Mechanised textile production concentrated workers into factory towns.
The steam engine, improved by James Watt, allowed factories to be sited
away from rivers and dramatically increased available power.
Urban populations grew rapidly and sanitation infrastructure lagged behind,
producing recurrent outbreaks of cholera in industrial cities.
UNIT TWO: TRADE AND EMPIRE
Expanding empires supplied raw materials and provided captive markets.
Railway construction reduced the cost of moving goods overland.
The telegraph allowed commercial information to travel faster than freight
for the first time in history.
UNIT THREE: LABOUR AND REFORM
Early factory legislation limited the working hours of children.
Trade unions were legalised only after sustained political campaigning.
The extension of the franchise proceeded gradually across the century.
Public health acts eventually mandated sewerage and clean water supply.
